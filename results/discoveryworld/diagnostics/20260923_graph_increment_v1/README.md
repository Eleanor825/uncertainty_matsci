# Graph-feature increment on recorded DiscoveryWorld trajectories

This **exploratory, post-outcome, read-only CPU audit** compares three previously frozen risk predictors on the same recorded candidates and official executed-action labels. Internal293 improves Brier, logloss and AUROC over NoGraph140 on both analyzed trajectories. GraphOnly153 performs best on the selected-G1 Full trajectory. These findings establish predictive differences on the observed records; they do not establish a causal online reduction in failed actions.

Each cohort is **one episode**, at Proteomics Normal world4/policy336/B30. Actions are serially correlated. G0 and the Full-trained selected G1 are kept separate. G1 changes the policy parameters relative to the G0 data used to train/calibrate the risk models, and the two controllers visit different trajectories; their risks and label prevalence must not be pooled. No row-level confidence intervals, significance tests, world-generalization claims, or family selection for future evaluation are made.

## Frozen-model comparison

All models predict `next_action_failure`, using the same original world0 fit set (60 rows, three episodes), world1 calibration set (60 rows, three episodes), original 200 updates and their own frozen temperature. They are Internal293 (temperature 2.655660205889806), NoGraph140 (4.047563757202568), and GraphOnly153 (2.481214627090778). The original ablation reproduced the production Internal tensor state and calibration. There was **no retraining, threshold/checkpoint search, new policy generation, graph extraction or environment interaction**. This CPU audit loaded three small NN models and evaluated 240 candidate-model rows.

Brier and logloss are lower-is-better; AUROC is higher-is-better. Logloss uses the frozen probability clip 1e-7. All 30 executed actions in each graph-producing cohort have common support for all three predictors, so no executed action was discarded there.

The general audit retains unsupported candidates and missing features separately; these data cannot reveal whether a separate NoGraph capture would have succeeded where full-graph capture failed.

Differences below subtract the second predictor from the first. All rows use the complete 30-action common-support set within their own episode.

The last row is retained: adding the non-graph feature family to GraphOnly did not improve this G1 episode's predictions. The G0 and G1 differences are descriptive results, not evidence that the graph contribution is invariant to policy updates.

Original recorded Internal probabilities were reproduced with maximum absolute error **0** for all 36 G0 and 44 G1 generated candidates. Recomputed Internal selections match the recorded selections on both episodes. There are 110 candidate rows total: 90 actually executed, labelled candidates and 20 unexecuted candidates with **empty outcome labels**. All observed candidate sets were generated by the original controller. Ranking disagreement does not tell us what would have happened had a different candidate been executed, or how a different controller would have generated its second proposal.

## Closed trajectory outcomes, as context

The original Full follow-up's three newly executed episodes all have final task score0: G0_UQ has4/30 failed actions, selected G1 without NN has18/30, and Full has16/30. The reused Native p336 baseline has29/30 failures and score0 in the [previously published complete B30 diagnostic](../20260923T125245_CST/README.md). These are the outcomes of the original registered policies; this offline audit does not create new policy outcomes or prove that its shadow NoGraph/GraphOnly choices improve them.
